
# Each campus has its rhythm. Plan for each campus not for aggregate

Say a program architect in Bangalore designs a 48-hour code sprint and mails all campuses: it starts this Friday. On one campus it lands perfectly. On another, university mid-semester exams start Monday, so the Campus Manager now has to choose between the sprint and the exams. On a third it is a state holiday and the labs are locked by university decree.

On the spreadsheet, the average campus was free. In reality, two out of three were set up to fail before the email was sent.

There is no average Kalvium campus. Each one has its own rhythm. One university holds exams in November, another in December. One region shuts down for Diwali, another for Pongal or Onam. One campus opens at 8, another has different entry rules entirely. Planning for the aggregate feels efficient because it fits in one row. It fails because no campus lives in that row.

Rhythm is about timing, not content. A campus can move when things happen. It cannot change what the program is. Student outcomes, the growth pillars, work integration and the growth hours are fixed. "In my campus rhythm we skip work integration" is not a timing adjustment, it is a change to the program itself, and that is not the campus's call.

If you are on the central team: design windows, not dates. "Run your 48-hour sprint between Jan 20 and 30, scheduled locally" works everywhere the fixed date did not. If your plan breaks on a predictable local constraint, that is a design failure, not an execution failure.

If you are on the campus team, you owe the other half. Map your constraints, exams, holidays, university events, well in advance and make them visible, because nobody in a central office can plan around an exam they do not know about. And once your rhythm is set, hold it. A campus that springs "we have a fest tomorrow" on the central team is creating the same failure in the other direction, and both sides end up planning blind.

Look at the last plan you sent out or received. Was it built for a real campus, or for the average one?
