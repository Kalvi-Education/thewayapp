
# Don't be emotional attached to a student but invest in their growth

A student is coasting this month: missing deadlines, arriving unprepared, going through the motions. His mentor has seen it for three weeks. Each time she considers naming it, she holds back. He has had a rough month, she does not want to pile on, she will give it one more week. One more week becomes the end of the term.

Nothing about that looks cruel in the moment. It looks like compassion. That is what makes attachment dangerous: in the moment it is indistinguishable from care, and the cost lands later, on the student.

The distinction to hold: attachment asks "how do I avoid hurting them today?", care asks "what will help them grow, even if it is uncomfortable?" Attach yourself to the student's long-term trajectory. Detach from their short-term discomfort, and from your own.

Detaching from the discomfort does not mean withdrawing the support. Naming the problem is the start of the work, not the end of it: sit with the student, understand what is actually in the way, a home situation, a knowledge gap, a fear, and work the plan with them while the standard stays where it is. Think of a scientist in a lab. Deeply invested in the experiment, genuinely wanting it to succeed, and yet when a hypothesis fails they do not treat it as a personal wound. They note the result, revise, run the next one. A mentor's job has the same shape. When your student fails an assessment or an internship, be there for them, and do not absorb the failure as your own emotional event. When their failure starts feeling like your failure, you will be tempted to protect yourself by going soft on them.

Attachment has three predictable costs. Burnout, because thirty students' failures landing personally is not survivable for years. Favouritism, because you check in more with the students you connect with, and stop seeing the cohort clearly. And lost honesty, because an attached mentor softens the exact feedback the student needed to change.

Which of your students has a problem you have known about for three weeks and not yet named to their face?
