
# Your effort, your outcome

Many students think that their marks or the institution they're from dictate their outcomes. This is not true.

One of the tenets of what we want to inculcate in students is: the industry and a person's career are very meritocratic. The effort one chooses to put in their work will determine the outcome they get. Our students are with us for 4 years. There is plenty of time to showcase this, and to call out how any outcome was due to the effort of a student. And any non-outcome was from the lack of effort. Students who put in the minimum to get a degree may only get that.

As you have yourself graduated, and learned this, use the opportunity to call this out to students via instances from the student's experience. Repeatedly doing this over 4 years is a key way of ensuring this life philosophy is truly learnt.

The instances are small and they are everywhere. The student who rewrote the submission nobody asked them to rewrite, and got the internship. The one who skipped the reviews all semester and could not explain their own project in the interview. Name it when it happens, while the student can still remember what they did or did not do. A general lecture on hard work at the end of 4 years teaches nothing.

Use this only where the effort was the deciding factor. If a student worked hard and still lost out, say that instead.
