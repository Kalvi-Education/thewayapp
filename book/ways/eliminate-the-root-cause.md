
# Eliminate the root cause. Ensure problems don't recur

The projector in the main classroom does not work, five minutes before a session. Someone runs and finds a cable, the class starts twelve minutes late, everyone moves on. Fine.

Now say it happens again the following month. And again after that. At what point does this stop being a projector problem?

The first time something breaks, it is an incident. You fix it. The second time, it is telling you about a system, and the system will keep producing it until somebody changes it. A campus where a projector fails every few weeks does not have bad projectors. It has nobody who owns equipment readiness, and it is paying for that in lost class time every month.

Firefighting is hard to give up because it is visible. You rescued the session. You found the laptop. You calmed the parent down. People thank you for it. Nobody thanks you for the meeting where you worked out why the fire keeps starting, even though that meeting saves far more class time.

The method is to keep asking why until the answer stops naming a person and starts naming a process. "The mentor forgot" is not a root cause, because people forget and will keep forgetting. "There is no checklist and no owner for session readiness" is a root cause, and you can fix that one once.

The next time you catch yourself fixing something familiar, stop in the middle of it and ask what would have to be true for this to never reach your desk again.
