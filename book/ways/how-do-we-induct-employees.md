
# How do we induct employees?

You are handed a new team member. What do the first 90 days look like?

The name we give the target is escape velocity. A rocket burns most of its fuel just leaving the ground, and induction is the same: heavy energy from you and from them early, so that by day 90 they run autonomously, aligned with how we work, adding value without supervision. Define what success at 30, 60 and 90 days looks like in the first week, together. A new person cannot hit a target they cannot see.

The method is the deep end with a safety net. Real problems with real stakes from week 1, not a bubble of theory, because this is how we teach students and it is how we teach ourselves. But lead every assignment with the why before the how. An instruction gets today's task done. The context behind it is what lets the person make a similar decision next month without asking you. And normalise "I don't know" in the first days. A new joiner who feels safe admitting a gap learns it, one who does not guesses, and builds on the guess.

Two things to watch for. Culture drops, meaning small deviations from how we work: hiding a mistake to look polished, speed over rigor, a partner treated transactionally. Call them out in the first week, while a correction is a small conversation and the habit is not yet set. And false alignment: people say yes to avoid looking slow, so have them explain the decision back in their own words before you trust the yes.

The daily journal is your window into all of this. Three lines from them: what I learnt, why we decided what we decided, where I need help. Your half of the deal is reading it and responding. A journal nobody responds to becomes a form to fill, and the honest entries stop.

Induction is the most labour-intensive thing a manager does here. The choice is whether you invest the 90 days now or carry the person for years.
