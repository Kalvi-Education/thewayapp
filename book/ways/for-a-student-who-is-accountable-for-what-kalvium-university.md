
# For a student, who is accountable for what - Kalvium, University, Company

When something goes wrong for a student, the first question is whose problem it is. Answer it lazily and each party assumes another is handling it, and the student falls through the middle. So name the map.

**Kalvium** is accountable for the program: curriculum, pedagogy, evaluation, mentorship, and the student's growth across the pillars. If the learning is broken, that is us, and no partner can be blamed for it.

**The university** is accountable for the ground: infrastructure, administration, compliance, degree requirements, and a campus life the student fully belongs to. A leaking lab roof or a certificate delay routes there, through the campus team, never through the student alone.

**The company**, once work integration begins, is accountable for real work and honest feedback. Not shadow projects, not silence. A student parked in a corner for a semester is a failure we take up with the company, because we do not trade student growth for a partnership.

**The parent** is accountable for the home half: backing the student through the demanding stretches, and telling us early when something at home is affecting the journey. A parent who hears about a problem in week twelve was failed by this map, not just by the student.

**The student** is accountable for the effort. Nobody on this map can put in their hours, sit their assessments, or build their skills for them.

The map earns its keep when an issue touches several parties at once. A student struggling at an internship involves Kalvium, the company and the student together, and without the map each waits for the other. With it, everyone knows their row: for instance the mentor coaches the student, Kalvium talks to the company, and the university is kept informed where the degree is affected.

Next time a student issue lands on you, place it on the map before you act. Whose row is it in, and who is waiting on you to route it?
