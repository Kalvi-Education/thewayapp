
# Mentor retention, transfer, and duty to students

"Why can't I move to Bangalore and work with the central team?" "I need a transfer to my home city, or I might have to resign."

In our early days we moved people often, sometimes to fill a vacancy, sometimes on request. It solved the immediate problem and created a quieter one: nobody invested in a place they expected to leave.

A tourist does not plant trees, because they will not be there when the trees are worth sitting under. When an educator is always eyeing the next exit, students sense it, and they stop investing back. They hold back the deeper struggles and the real aspirations, because they do not want to re-explain themselves to the next person, and the one after.

Staying works differently, and it compounds. A mentor in their second year with the same batch does not just know the data, they know that one student's logic collapses under stress and another's overconfidence covers a fear of failing. Coaching at that depth is simply unavailable to a newcomer. The campus keeps its memory: the feel of the lab, the relationship with the registrar, the local culture. And to a student or parent, you are Kalvium. If the face keeps changing, the program feels unstable no matter how good the curriculum is.

This cuts both ways, and neither side of it is about forbidding movement. Transfers happen: for growth, for family, for reasons that matter. What we hold on our side is the intent to stop moving people for short-term convenience, and to invest in your growth on your campus, so that staying is a career and not a sacrifice. What we ask on yours: treat joining a campus as signing up for those students' journey, and weigh a move against what it does to them, not only against what it does for you.

And when life eventually moves you on, leave responsibly. A responsible exit hands over the care, not just the files: who needs a push, who needs a pat, what the campus needs next. Sit with whoever follows you until they hold the stories.

Think about the batch you teach now. What are you building with them that only pays off in their third year, and who will be holding it then?
