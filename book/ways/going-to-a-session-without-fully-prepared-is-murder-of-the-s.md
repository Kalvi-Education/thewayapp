
# Going to a session unprepared is murder of the session

"I'm just going to wing it today."

Imagine a surgeon saying that. The patient is prepped, the clock is ticking, and the surgeon tells the nurse: I didn't look at the scans, but I've done plenty of surgeries, I'll open him up and figure it out. Would you let that surgeon touch someone you love?

A session is not life-or-death, but it is life-shaping. An unprepared mentor operates on 30 career trajectories without a map: fuddling through concepts, giving vague answers to sharp questions, leaving students more confused than they arrived.

The math is what earns this page its title. Stand in front of 30 students for one hour and you consume 30 hours of human life, more than a full day of existence, concentrated into one session. Walk in unprepared and that day produces confusion instead of learning. There is no retake. That exact hour, with those students at that point in their journey, does not come again. And a bad session is not neutral, it digs a trust deficit that takes 3 to 4 good sessions to refill.

Being prepared means three layers. The story: why this subject exists and where today's learning unit sits in it, so you can teach the why and not just the how. The student: where this class has come from and what they are likely to be stuck on this week. The things: projector, laptop, connectors, working code, before the hour starts, because fumbling with equipment eats learning time like anything else.

Preparation is also what cures session anxiety. Feeling restless before facing sharp students is common, and the fix is not confidence tricks. Finish the learning unit as a learner, anticipate the pitfalls, rehearse your part. The anxiety drops because the unknowns did.

Before your next session, run the check: do I know the story, do I know the students, do the things work?
