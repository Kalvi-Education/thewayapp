
# Nothing will compensate for lack of professionalism

We had a student, unnamed for this story. If you asked anyone in the classroom, or their mentor, who would be the most proficient student, most would have called out his name. When he started working in a team, he was able to get started very quickly.

But then, he started missing standups. Usually he would have very good reasons. Then the work did not follow timelines. The end of the day PR came next day, with a very good reason, when asked. Messages sent to him were not responded to immediately.

This internship was not renewed by the company. The feedback was "Not reliable".

Close your eyes and imagine a "professional". You probably imagined a person in formal clothes, speaking very articulately. But that's just like thinking being a doctor is wearing a white coat and speaking medical jargon.

Being a professional means understanding what role you play in the people surrounding you, and ensuring you're doing all of that reliably. You show up for every meeting on time, fully prepared. You understand the work given to you, and if not, ensure that's the first thing you do. You hold a high bar on the quality of the work you do. You do not pass off something that it is not. And you do all of this while keeping emotions of frustration, boredom, anger out, because that would impact what others can do with your work.

Being a good professional means knowing your role, and doing all aspects of that fully. Wearing a coat or a tie is just the surface.

One of our core team put it this way: talent and skill are transient, fungible, and developable in a short time. Character and professionalism are not. A company can teach a new framework in a few weeks. It cannot teach someone to care about their own word.

So when you next see a student who is the best in the room technically, ask yourself what their feedback would say after six months in a company.
