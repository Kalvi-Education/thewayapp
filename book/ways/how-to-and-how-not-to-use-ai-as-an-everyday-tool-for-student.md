
# How to (and how not to) use AI as an everyday tool (for students)

You learned long division even though calculators existed. Log tables, probability, statistics by hand, all of it doable by machine before you were born. Was that wasted time?

No, because the purpose was never producing the answer. It was building the understanding underneath, the number sense that later lets you catch a wrong answer, estimate, reason. A student who did every math assignment on a calculator would have produced correct answers for years while learning almost nothing, because the point of the assignment was what doing it built in you.

What the calculator was to arithmetic, AI now is to writing, coding, research, most of what our students do in a day. It is all around them and will be for the rest of their working lives, so banning it would be as silly as banning calculators, and we do not. Instead we hand students one question to run every single time: is this AI helping me understand, or helping me escape understanding?

Stuck on a concept and asking AI to explain it three different ways: understanding. In the service of your education. Pasting the assignment in and submitting what comes back: escape. The work got done and the student did not grow, which for someone who came here to be transformed is a strange thing to pay for. Notice that the tool did not decide which of those happened. How the student used it did.

We are deliberately not writing a rulebook here, no approved-tools list, no banned-uses list. The world is still working out what humans and AI are to each other, and rules written this year are unlikely to survive contact with next year. The calculator question is a parable, and like any parable you can extend it to cases nobody has imagined yet. What we will say plainly is what is at stake: critical thinking. Lean on AI as a crutch, skip the understanding, and the thinking that AI is supposed to amplify is the thing that stopped developing.

Watch how a student uses AI this week and ask them the question. Do they already know their answer?
