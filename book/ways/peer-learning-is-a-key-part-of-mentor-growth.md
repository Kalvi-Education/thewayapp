
# Peer learning is a key part of mentor growth

We drill GPS into students: Google, Peer, Seek support. Do not struggle in silence. Then a mentor hits a wall with a difficult module and struggles in silence for a week, because asking might look like weakness.

The same logic we teach applies to us. Transforming students takes technical depth, high energy and emotional intelligence, and no single mentor has all three, every day, for every student. In many organizations people hoard knowledge to protect their status. Here that is a failure mode. There is no competition among mentors, only collaboration: if you solve a problem, share it, and if you are stuck, ask.

Two of our own instances show why. When mentors started comparing notes before parent meetings, one discovered that a student he found difficult was thriving with another mentor, who had simply found the approach that worked. The answer had been on the campus for weeks, with a colleague he had not asked. And when Discrete Mathematics entered the curriculum and became a hurdle for many, one mentor built a resource base and shared it, lifting ten mentors with one person's effort.

Collaboration that depends on personality does not survive scale, so it needs structures. Circle time where mentors discuss challenges openly. Peer demos. Sitting in a colleague's session to learn rather than judge. A shared resource pool for a hard module. If your campus has none of these, that is not a reason to wait, it is the gap to raise with your Campus Manager and start filling, one ritual at a time. Our side of it: developing mentors is Kalvium's duty, not a side effect, and structures like these are how we intend to meet it.

What it costs is ego. Invite someone into your classroom. When a peer suggests a better way to explain recursion, they have just improved every session you run from here on. Take it that way.
