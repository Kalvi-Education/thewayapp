
# Integrity at Kalvium

A student has not understood the assignment, and it is due tonight. A working version is one paste away. Nobody would know.

Walk both paths forward. The student who submits what they have and says "I did not get the logic" takes a lower score, gets guidance, and owns that concept from then on. The student who pastes passes tonight, and the gap in understanding stays exactly where it was. Two months later the same concept comes up in an internship interview, with a probing question behind it, and there is no help available and no time left to learn. The panic in that room was set up two months earlier, at the paste.

This is the distinction we hold. A low score or a missed deadline is a failure of effort, temporary and fixable. Cheating is a different kind of failure. It converts a small problem you could have solved into a debt that comes due at a worse time, with higher stakes.

The same mechanics apply to a lie inside a team. "Almost done" when you have not started buys one calm day. The team plans the sprint on your word and the delivery collapses. The code gets fixed in a week. Your word takes longer, because from now on people double-check your estimates before planning on them. "I am stuck", said early, keeps the problem small.

And the debt is not always personal. When a company catches a student in malpractice during a placement drive, it has no way to know how rare that was. The safe assumption, for them, is to trust the next Kalvium batch a little less. Batchmates and future batches pay for that.

Our side of the deal: a student who raises a flag early gets guidance, not humiliation. If admitting a gap costs a student more than hiding it, we built that incentive, and we fix it. This page is about that student, the one honest mistake away from a bad decision. Deliberate and repeated cheating is a different matter, and it goes through the conduct process.
