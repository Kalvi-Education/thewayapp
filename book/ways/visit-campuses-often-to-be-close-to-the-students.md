
# Visit campuses often to be close to the students

On a dashboard, one of our campuses had "internet connectivity issues". A ticket. A logistical item to resolve over email.

Then someone from the central team visited that campus at the peak of summer. The power had just gone out, the generators had not kicked in, the ACs were off, and the internet was down. Students were trying to write code while sweating, swatting mosquitoes, unable to open documentation. The visitor's conclusion was immediate: you cannot expect quality work from a human being in this room. The item stopped being "fix the WiFi" and became "fix the environment", at a different priority.

Both versions came from the same facts. There is an analogy often attributed to Elon Musk: someone can tell you the stove is hot, and you can even believe them, but until your own hand feels the heat you will not respect it. The dashboard reported the outage. The visit made it impossible to deprioritise.

If you design programs, products, curriculum or policy, every metric you use is a proxy for some ground reality, and proxies drift. The longer it has been since you stood in a Kalvium classroom, the more you are designing for a campus that exists in your head. The way to check whether a number still means what it meant is to go and watch the behaviour behind it.

Visits are for more than finding problems. Go to test an assumption your design depends on. Go to try an idea on the people who will live with it. Go to know the mentors and the Campus Manager as people, because the phone call that averts a crisis next semester is built on that.

One caution. A visit is expensive for the campus, not for you. You take the Campus Manager's attention, the mentors' time, the students' space. So go as an observer, not a chief guest. Know what you came to learn before you land, watch quietly, share what you found with the campus team before you leave, and take at least one action home with your name on it.

When did you last feel the stove, rather than read its temperature?
