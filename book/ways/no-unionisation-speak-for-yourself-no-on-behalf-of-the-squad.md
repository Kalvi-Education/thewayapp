
# No Unionisation - Speak for yourself - No "On behalf of the squad"

"On behalf of the entire batch, we feel this deadline is impossible."

A student once came to a mentor with this line. Call him Rahul. "The general sentiment is that we cannot do it," he said. The mentor tested the general sentiment. She called three other students from the batch. One had already finished most of the work. One said it was tight but possible. One admitted he had not started and was hoping for an extension.

The batch Rahul described did not exist. Three calls found three different situations, and the claimed consensus was really one student's situation wearing fifty students' names.

This is why we ask you to speak as yourself. "I am struggling with this deadline" is a sentence we can act on. We can look at your situation, on its merits, and help. "Everyone feels this is unfair" flattens fifty different situations into one, and the one it most resembles is usually the speaker's own. We grow students individually, so we need the individual case.

Speaking for a crowd also costs the speaker something. A request made as "everyone" belongs to no one, so nobody follows it up and nobody learns from its answer. The skill a working life runs on is the opposite one: state your own case, plainly, to the person who can act on it, and own what comes back.

Real systemic issues exist, and one student is sometimes the first to spot one. Bring it as yourself, to your mentor or the Campus Manager: "here is what I am seeing, and I think it affects others too". If more students bring the same thing independently, that tells us far more than any claimed consensus could. And a group of students genuinely affected by the same thing can absolutely all raise it. Each one speaking for themselves is what makes it credible.

Our side: a student who comes alone and speaks honestly must find that it works. If speaking as "I" gets a worse hearing than speaking as "we", we built the wrong incentive.
