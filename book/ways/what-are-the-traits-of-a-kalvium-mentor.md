
# What are the traits of a Kalvium mentor?

Skills can be trained. Traits are what a person brings into the room before any training starts, and five of them make a Kalvium mentor.

**Ownership.** "I covered the material but they didn't learn it" is a sentence a Kalvium mentor cannot say. The outcome you own is the learning, not the coverage. If a student is stuck, that is your problem until it is solved.

**A growth mindset toward students.** You believe every student in front of you can be developed, including the ones currently drifting. The moment you privately write a student off as "just not capable", you stop looking for what would move them, and your behaviour towards them starts showing it.

**Professional modelling.** You are the first working professional many of these students will know closely. On time, prepared, crisp in communication, honest about what you do not know, because they learn how a professional behaves by watching you.

**System responsibility.** Your classroom sits inside a larger system. When something upstream is broken, a bad question, a clashing schedule, a confusing policy, you surface it and push for the fix rather than shrugging it off as someone else's department.

**Continuity.** Transformation happens over months of consistent coaching, not in a weekend. Changing a mentor mid-journey is like changing a coach mid-season, so staying with your students through the cycle is itself a trait, a commitment you make walking in.

Notice none of the five is technical brilliance. Teaching craft and technical depth can be trained in months. These five are the slow part, which is why we look for them at the door and work to protect them after.

Score yourself honestly against the five. Which one would your students say is your weakest?
