
# Grow the people around me

Education is our product. We tell every student that we will grow them. We cannot credibly be that place if, inside it, only the students grow. So this is an expectation of every employee, not a nice-to-have: the people around you should be measurably better because you are there.

There is plain arithmetic behind it too. Kalvium is growing, which means bigger responsibilities keep appearing. You step up only when someone near you can hold what you hold today, so growing them is also how you make your own next role possible. The same holds for students in a squad: they learn from each other daily, in reviews, debugging sessions and overheard arguments, so if the squad's overall level stays flat, even the strongest student in it grows less.

Growing someone has two ingredients, and both are needed. A stretch goal, work slightly past what they can already do. And psychological safety, the certainty that an honest failure on that stretch gets examined, not punished. Stretch without safety and people hide their struggles from you. Safety without stretch and nobody leaves what they already know.

Know the difference between growing and rescuing. When a colleague is drowning and you quietly do their work, you have not grown them. Help them plan the work, review their draft before it ships, stay reachable while they do it. The doing stays theirs, because the doing is where the growth is.

And do not read "around me" as "below me". Unblocking a peer, or a senior, grows the organization exactly as much. "Not my team" is not a reason.

This is also what leadership means here. We are not leaders of teams, we are a team of leaders, and we promote from within because it is easier to coach skill than to instil soul. The test of your leadership is observable: take a week off, and watch what stalls. Whatever stalls is what you have not yet grown someone into.

Who is better at their job this quarter because of you? If no name comes to mind, whose stretch goal could you set this week?
