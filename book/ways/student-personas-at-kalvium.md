
# Student personas at Kalvium

Two students miss the same deadline. One has been in the lab until closing every night. The other has barely appeared this month. If your response to both is the same, one of them got the wrong response.

So before you respond to anything a student did, place them on the Effort x Mastery map for this month: how much are they putting in, and how much is converting to capability? The two answers give you one of four states, and the state, not the incident, tells you what to do.

Diagnosing takes evidence, not impressions. Effort you can see in the lab hours, the submissions, the questions asked. Mastery you can see in whether the work stands up when probed. Check both, because each state has a lookalike. A student striving this month looks lazy if you only saw the failed assessment, and what they need is help finding the hidden gap, often one they are afraid to reveal, plus confidence. Push them to work harder and you add anxiety to a student already at risk of burnout. A student coasting this term looks like your best student if you only saw the marks. The miss is in the process they are skipping, and the ask is reframing what they are here for, because the skipped habits are what a team will demand of them. A student drifting this month can pass as quietly fine if nobody is looking at either axis. Diagnose why the disengagement before any plan, they are the ones the program is at real risk of not working for. And a student thriving this month is the easiest to misread of all, because the misread is to stop paying attention. They need bigger challenges and room to lift peers, and watching for perfectionism and burnout.

Re-diagnose at a cadence. States move month to month, and a response calibrated to last semester's state can be exactly wrong for the state the student is in now.
