
# Avoid knee-jerk , review at a cadence

"The standup is pointless. Let's scrap it."

That message went around a group chat after a noisy week on one campus. One student had complained, one mentor had agreed, and within hours there was a proposal to dismantle something that had taken months to become culture. We did not scrap it. The campus was going through a rough patch that week, and once the week passed, so did the noise. The standup stayed, and it was doing its job.

The pull toward fast change comes from a good place. People here care, and when you care, one complaint feels like a crisis. But a single complaint is one data point. A pattern is the same thing surfacing from different people, unprompted, over time. The knee-jerk response treats the first like the second.

There is also a cost that stays invisible until you look for it. For a student, stability is part of the experience. A rule that changed overnight teaches every student that any rule might change overnight, and the energy they spend wondering what changes tomorrow was supposed to go into learning.

So we work on cadences. Give everything you run a review point, a moment where it gets examined with real data across the cohort and mentor feedback, not one loud anecdote. When a concern arrives between reviews, acknowledge it immediately and act on it at the review: "noted, we will look at this with the full data when we review". The concern is heard now and answered when there is enough signal to answer wisely.

The exception is real harm. A safety issue, a student at risk, a mentor in crisis: move now. The cadence is for systems, not emergencies.

Next time you feel the urge to change a process today, ask what you are holding: one report, a pattern, or harm. Only the third one is a reason to move today.
