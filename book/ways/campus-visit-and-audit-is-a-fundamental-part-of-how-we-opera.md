
# Campus visit and audit is a fundamental part of how we operate.

We run on trust. Campus Managers hold real autonomy, mentors run their own classrooms, and nobody waits for head office to approve the day. The principle that keeps that workable is trust, but verify.

So we visit campuses often and we audit them regularly. A campus drifts in small ways, a shortcut here, a skipped ritual there, and the people inside it stop noticing because they see it every day. A visitor seeing the campus fresh notices in an hour what the resident team has absorbed as normal.

The frame matters. An audit here is not an inspection where someone passes or fails. It is the mechanism by which a Campus Manager and their reporting manager continuously improve that campus. The output of a good audit is not a score, it is a short list of what this campus improves next, agreed with the team.

If you run a campus, the audit exists to improve your campus, and you should be using it that way, asking the visitor what they saw. If you are the visitor, come to improve the campus, and leave the campus team knowing exactly what improves next.

When someone last walked your campus with fresh eyes, what did they see that you had stopped seeing?
