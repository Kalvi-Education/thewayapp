
# Learn to love what you do

"I want to do Data Science. Why am I being asked to work on backend systems?"

Students arrive with a wishlist of technologies, usually assembled from LinkedIn trends and job-title glamour. They want the label. What is missing underneath the label is the thing this work actually runs on: solving a real problem for a real person with technology.

One of our students was set on Data Science. When an internship came from a global retailer that did not match that vision, he hesitated: hold out for the label, or solve the problem in front of him? He took the work, and found the data problems he craved sitting inside the larger challenge of how a retailer that size operates. He arrived waiting for the right task and left looking for the right problem.

The reasoning matters more than the story. A tech stack is a tool for reaching a problem, and the problems outlast every stack. A student who is here mainly to learn syntax has picked a shrinking target, because AI tools now write much of it. A student who is here only for the paycheque will find the rigor of this industry outlasts that motivation. What survives is caring about what the work does: code that gets a farmer a loan, or catches a diagnosis earlier, or quietly makes a system faster for everyone who touches it. A student who cares about that outcome experiences the late debugging night as craft. The one who does not experiences the same night as punishment.

Tools can check whether the code runs. What only you can do is connect the grind to what it unlocks: when a student is deep in a hard concept, show them the real thing it makes possible. Move them from "I have to do this" to "I want to see if I can solve this".

Which of your students is currently chasing a label? What problem could you put in front of them instead?
