
# Focus on what the student needs over what they want

"Sir, why do we have to do this reflection journal? I already wrote the code. It works."

You are going to hear versions of this often. The student wants to skip the journal. The student wants the deadline moved because the week was heavy. The student wants the interesting tech and not the communication session. What do you say?

Saying yes is easy, and it feels good. The tension leaves the room, the student likes you, your feedback scores go up, and nothing bad happens today.

Think about the difference between a spa and a gym. In a spa the customer is comfortable, and if they leave sweating, something has gone wrong. In a gym the customer is paying you to make them do the thing they would not do alone, and a trainer who hands them a lighter weight so they will be happy has taken their money and given them nothing. We are a gym. When we say student-first, we mean the student's transformation comes first, and the resistance is part of the design.

There is a particular trap for new mentors, which is wanting to be the one the students like. A student complains about the rigor and you say "I know, these rules are a bit much, just get through it." You have bought their affection by teaching them the difficulty is pointless, when the difficulty is what they came here for.

A student who misses deadlines sprint after sprint does not need one more extension. They need to learn to run their own week, and each extension pushes that lesson out by another sprint while the interviews get closer. A genuine emergency is a different thing, and telling the two apart is part of your job.

Holding the line comes with our side of the deal. The student gets the why, not just the no: what the journal is for, what the difficulty is building. Rigor with no explanation reads as arbitrary, and a student who thinks the difficulty is arbitrary is right to push against it.

We are not here to make students happy every day. We are here to make them proud of who they have become when they look back. Which of this week's requests will you say no to, and can you give the why when you do?
