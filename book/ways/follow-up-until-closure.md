
# Follow up until closure

A mentor at one of our campuses bought whiteboard markers out of petty cash and emailed his manager for reimbursement. The manager was travelling, or busy, or just missed it. The mentor had done his part, so he left it there.

Three weeks later the campus fund was short by that exact amount, and he could not buy drinking water cans. He found out when a few students came and told him the can was empty.

The manager missed the email, and the mentor missed the outcome. One reminder would have closed it.

We tell ourselves reasonable things to avoid sending that reminder. "I told them once, now it's their job." "They didn't reply, so this is not on me." And the one we hear most on our campuses, because people here are genuinely nice: "They must be busy, let me not disturb them." In each case the work stays open, and whoever is closest to the impact pays for it, usually a student.

So the rule we work by is that whoever feels the impact owns the loop. Owning it means following the thing until it is resolved, not until you have acted on it. You remind. You remind again. If it still does not move, you escalate. Escalating a stuck item is not a complaint about a colleague.

A manager here once said, "If it is my problem, I will follow up even with God."

Think about what is sitting open right now that you have already told yourself is somebody else's job.
