
# Short-term compromise harms the long-term goal

We once expanded to a new campus faster than we were ready to. The infrastructure was incomplete, the mentor team was thin, onboarding had gaps. There was pressure to move, and moving felt more important than being ready. That campus struggled for two semesters, never reached the quality bar we held elsewhere, and the university relationship eventually frayed beyond repair. The shortcut cost more time and more relationship capital than doing it right would have.

First, the definition, because not every concession is a compromise. A sub-optimal choice made under genuine constraint, with the tradeoff assessed honestly, is a decision. A compromise is when we know the right standard and choose not to hold it, because holding it is slow, unpopular or inconvenient. It rarely announces itself. The phrases it usually arrives in are "just this once", "we'll fix it later", "the situation is exceptional", "let's be pragmatic". Sometimes those phrases are accurate. They are also the standard packaging of decisions we later regret.

Compromises on student standards break the most. We once relaxed a professionalism threshold for work-integration eligibility. The margin was small and the pressure was real. Next cycle there was a ruckus, from students who had been denied on similarly slim margins earlier. They said the standard had been arbitrary, and they were right, we had just made it arbitrary. That is the mechanism to hold onto: an exception, once granted, becomes an argument available to the next case, and the standard stops being a standard the moment it is negotiable.

The lens that helps in the moment: is this decision reversible? Reversible ones tolerate experiments, move fast and correct course. Irreversible ones, a culture-damaging hire, a partnership on unsustainable terms, a student exception that becomes precedent, deserve the slow answer. If the cost of being wrong is structural, hold the standard.

Which pressure are you carrying right now that is quietly asking for a "just this once"?
