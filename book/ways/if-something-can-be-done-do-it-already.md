
# If something can be done, do it already

A ticket comes in. You read it. Twenty minutes of work, you have the context, nothing blocks you. You close the tab: "I'll get to it tomorrow."

One of our core team put the cost plainly: you are closest to the context of the moment. Five days later you may lose some of that. Doing it tomorrow means reading the ticket again, reconstructing what you had already worked out, and finding the same files a second time. The twenty-minute task quietly becomes a forty-minute one, and until it is done it sits on your list, taking up a little of your attention every time you look at it.

This trap is easy to fall into when your queue mixes small tasks with interesting problems. The problems are energising, the tasks are not, so the tasks sit. But those small tasks are often other people's unblocked work, and they will come due eventually, usually in the week you can least afford them.

The boundary is clarity. If the situation is clear, the solution is clear, nothing blocks you and the task is yours, do it now, because now is when it is cheapest. If any of those is missing, this rule does not apply, and the right move is the clarity conversation, not a rushed guess. Acting fast without clarity is the knee-jerk, and there is a separate page on that.

Count what is on your list right now that is clear, unblocked, yours, and under half an hour. Why is it still on the list?
