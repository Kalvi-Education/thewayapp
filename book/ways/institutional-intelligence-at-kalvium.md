
# Institutional intelligence at Kalvium

A Campus Manager who has run their campus for three semesters moves on. They knew which university office actually processes requests, which week of the semester attendance dips, what went wrong with the last event and how they fixed it. None of it was written down, so their successor spends six months rediscovering each one, making the same mistakes the campus already paid for once.

A lesson Kalvium learns lives, at first, in one person's head. Institutional intelligence is the work of moving it out: what we tried, what failed, why, and what we do now instead, written where the next person will find it. We apply this discipline to students through Student 360, the aggregated record of what we know about each student. This page is about applying the same discipline to what we know about running Kalvium itself.

Why it matters more here than at most places: we are growing. More campuses, more people changing roles, and an unwritten lesson has to be relearned by every campus that hits the same problem. This book itself is the same discipline at work, it exists so that what we learned building Kalvium does not stay locked in the heads of the people who built it.

The habit is small. When something surprises you, when a fix works, when a decision goes wrong in an instructive way, capture it where your team will look, not in a document nobody opens again.

Run this test on yourself. If you left tomorrow, what would your successor most wish you had written down? That is your backlog. Start it this week.
