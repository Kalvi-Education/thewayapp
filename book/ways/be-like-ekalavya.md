
# Be like Ekalavya.

"The university hasn't updated the portal." "My mentor hasn't given me feedback yet." "When will the placement team bring the companies?"

Each of these sentences might be true. Notice what they share: in all three, the student has decided their progress sits in someone else's hands, and is waiting.

Ekalavya was refused by the teacher he wanted. He built a clay figure of that teacher, practised in front of it daily, and became a master archer. The usual reading is a story about talent. The better reading is about agency: he did not treat the refusal as permission to stop.

Note what Ekalavya still needed. The forest, the clay, the routine, a master to model himself on. Agency is not a rejection of mentorship or structure. Our mentors, classrooms and reviews exist because guided learning is faster than lone learning. The point is what you do in the gaps. A student with agency asks for feedback before it is scheduled, finds a second source when the first is delayed, builds the portfolio nobody assigned. A student without it learns only what is handed over, exactly when it is handed over.

Why we insist on this: the industry our students enter moves faster than any curriculum can. A syllabus is written once a year. The tools change far more often than that. Some of what we teach will age out, and the skill of learning on your own initiative is what keeps a career running when it does.

Two different things get mixed up here, so separate them for your students. A blocked system is real: portal down, feedback missing, report it and escalate it. But while the ticket is open, the student can still write the code, review the material, ask a peer. The portal being down stops the submission. It does not stop the learning.

When a student tells you they are waiting for something, ask them what they did while waiting. If the answer is nothing, that is the thing to coach: walk them through what was available to them during the wait, and ask for it the next time they are blocked.
