
# Who is a prospective Kalvium student? (Selection)

Admission season. Picture two applicants. One has the higher board score and answers every question with what he thinks you want to hear. The other scored lower, taught herself to build a small website last summer, and asks you what happens when a student falls behind. Which one belongs here?

Marks alone cannot answer that, which is why we do not select on marks alone. We do not admit students because they need a college. We admit them because they are ready to show up, with hunger, discipline and curiosity. The selection looks for three things underneath the scores: grit, does this student keep going when something is hard. Self-awareness, do they know what they are weak at and say so plainly. Capacity to grow, is there a gap between what they are and what they could become that effort would close. A top scorer can have all three. A top scorer can also have none of them, and a school career where the work came easily will not have tested any of them.

So we use assessments, psychometrics, academic history and interviews together, and we treat the interview as a place to find these signals, not to intimidate. The purpose of selection is not gatekeeping for its own sake. It is to find those who will thrive here, and to protect the experience for everyone else. Students who arrive wanting shortcuts pull at the culture the rest of the classroom depends on.

That protection cuts both ways, and this is the uncomfortable part: we have to be okay saying no. No when the pressure is a target, a channel partner, or a parent who insists. A yes given for one of those reasons is a promise we already know we cannot keep.

When you next assess an applicant, what are you actually reading, the scorecard or the student?
