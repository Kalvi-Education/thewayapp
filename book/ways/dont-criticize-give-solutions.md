
# Don't criticise, give solutions

A thousand things happen in a classroom every day, and statistically one or two of them will not be ideal. A session runs late. A grade is posted wrong. A mentor has an off day.

Each of those can go two ways. It can become a note to the person who can fix it, with a suggestion attached. Or it can become a story, told sideways, in the hostel, in a group chat, sometimes on social media, growing a little with each retelling. The first route fixes the thing. The second route makes a mountain out of a molehill, and now the team is managing the mountain instead of fixing the molehill.

So the practice we hold, for students and for ourselves: bring the criticism to someone who can act on it, and bring your best attempt at a fix along. "This session did not work, and here is what would have worked for me" is a contribution, and understanding the system well enough to propose a fix is itself half the learning. "This session did not work" repeated to twenty people who cannot change it fixes nothing, and it muddies the water for everybody.

The boundary: not every problem comes with a solution attached, and that is fine. If something looks unsafe, or broken in a way you cannot diagnose, say so to the person who can act, plainly, without waiting until you have the fix. What this practice asks is that the complaint travel toward action, not away from it.

Our side: a solution offered has to be taken seriously. If people bring fixes and watch them vanish, they will go back to just complaining, and they will be right to.
