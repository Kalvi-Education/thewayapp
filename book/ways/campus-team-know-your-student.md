
# Campus Team know your student

"A parent called about Daniel. Let me check the dashboard to see how he's doing." "Khushi? I'll have to check which squad she is in."

If it takes a day to learn the background of a student who sits twenty feet away, ask what the physical campus is for. Learning material is a commodity now. A student with YouTube, documentation and AI can learn to code without meeting another human. Parents do not leave their children with us for our dashboards. They believe that able professionals are personally watching their child grow, and that if they call, someone will know the story, not just the scores.

So we hold a concrete standard, the rule of knowledge. For every student, at least two mentors on campus know their full story: technical blockers, professional habits, and the aspirations the student has chosen to share. Each mentor deeply knows 25 to 35 students. The Campus Manager knows 80 to 100 by name and current state, where current state means what you can observe, effort, attendance, recent struggles, not a feeling. When an exam happens, the CM should know who was struggling before the report says so.

Knowing the story is a professional duty with a professional boundary. What a student shares about their life informs how you coach them. It is not campus conversation material, and it does not travel further than the people who need it to help.

The tools serve this, they do not replace it. HEROS, our student record system, and the reflection journals hold the story: what a 1-1 covered, the conversation behind a disciplinary action, the small wins. Kept well, they make the next conversation better. Kept as compliance, they are the dashboard the parent call gets deflected to.

And when a mentor leaves or a student changes squads, treat the handover as a baton exchange, not a data transfer. The incoming mentor does a round of 1-1s and takes notes from the outgoing one, so the student does not start their story over.

Pick a student you have not spoken to in a month. Who on this campus knows their story right now, by name?
