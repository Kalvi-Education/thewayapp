
# Embrace that life can be unfair

"Sir, I solved 3 questions in the Morgan Stanley assessment and my friend solved 2. He got shortlisted for the interview and I didn't. Why?"

We hear often from students that call out "unfairness" in various events that happen in and outside the classroom. Usually they have been trained by their school or college thus far. Do this, and you get that. Get these marks, and this rank. This is manufactured fairness.

But we run an undergraduate program, and 95% of the students will graduate out of formal education, and are going to find out that fairness isn't a property of the world. It is our job to help students learn this.

Rohit Sharma lost 14 international match tosses in a row. That coin did not like him or hate him. The wind, a butterfly far away, and the umpire's thumb flick did not either. They all just led to that outcome. What advice would you give Rohit Sharma in this situation?

Whatever you would tell him, tell the student in front of you. The toss is lost. The week spent appealing to a referee is also lost. There is a next match to play.

Some things are in our control, and some things not in our control. When our own process wrongs a student, a wrong grade, a missed communication, a broken commitment, we fix it. Teaching students this does not mean we do nothing for students, especially things in our control.
