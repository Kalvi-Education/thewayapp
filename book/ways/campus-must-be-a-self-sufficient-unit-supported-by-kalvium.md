
# A campus must be a self-sufficient unit supported by Kalvium

A projector dies during an assessment. A mentor calls in sick on a teaching day. A university officer changes a room booking an hour before a session. None of these can wait for a reply from a central office, and none of them should need one.

We run each campus as a self-sufficient unit. The campus team, led by the Campus Manager, handles its own day: scheduling, continuity plans for disruptions, local relationships, the everyday problems that come with running a building full of students. Anticipate issues, and when you escalate one, come with two or three viable solutions, not just the problem.

Self-sufficient does not mean alone. The central teams exist to serve the campus: curriculum, systems, tools, enablement. That is support, and you should demand it loudly when something central is broken. What the campus does not outsource is judgment about its own ground. The people in a central office are not the ones who know your students, your mentors and your university partner. You are.

The line to hold: timings, room plans and local arrangements are yours to adapt. The program, the standards and the outcomes are not.
