
# We don't do work for the sake of optics

We once ran a stretch of placement drives that looked great in updates. Company after company, drive after drive, plenty of activity to report. The roles had little overlap with our students' skills. No placements came out of it. The work was real, the effort was real, and none of it moved a student forward.

That is optics work: effort arranged to be seen rather than to change an outcome. It shows up quietly. A mentor chasing feedback scores instead of checking whether the class learnt anything. An event presented as "10% improvement over last month" using whichever metric happened to improve. Doing just enough to avoid scrutiny from the head office. The report gets filed either way. What the student got out of it is the part that varies.

Two things make this hard to root out, and both are ours to fix.

First, optics grow where people believe raw reality will be punished. That belief is rational in plenty of organisations, so we have to make it irrational here. When a drive fails, we say it failed and what we learnt, without wrapping it in a positive. A team that can report a failure plainly has no reason to decorate one.

Second, real outcomes can be slow. A student's growth can sit visibly unchanged through weeks of honest work on it. If we only reward what shows this month, we are training people to manufacture things that show. So a manager here must be able to look at effort tied to a long-term outcome and say, out loud, that it is on track even though the needle has not moved yet.

None of this makes visibility bad. When an outcome is real, showcase it widely. The test is only the order: did the outcome produce the showcase, or did the need for a showcase produce the work?

Look at your last weekly update. How much of it changed something for a student?
