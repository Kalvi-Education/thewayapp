
# Showing up every day is a pre-requisite

"Sir, I have a laptop and WiFi. Why do I need to come to the lab to push code to GitHub? I can do this from my hostel room."

Every mentor gets asked this. The answer you give decides what kind of program you are running.

We are unapologetic about our timings: 8 hours a day, 9 to 6, Monday to Saturday. While others may choose other timings, we have a specific purpose, skilling up students so they are industry ready. You can learn some skills on your own, but the skill to show up regularly and put in the work can only be learned by showing up regularly and putting in the work. No exceptions.

As Kalvium our role becomes: ensure that time put in by students is used productively. If something is broken in what we are doing, and it is not helping the student, then call it out and seek changes.

That second half is the part many mentors forget. A student sitting in a lab for 8 hours with nothing useful to do learns a different lesson than the one we intended. Before you hold a student to the schedule, check that the schedule is holding up its end.

There is also the question of what showing up means. A student swiping in at 9 and scrolling reels for two hours is not present. Neither is one walking in and out every twenty minutes, or sitting through a session with headphones on. Presence is attention, and attention is what converts 8 hours into skill.

So when a student asks you why they cannot do this from their hostel room, answer them properly. Then check whether today, on your campus, the 8 hours you asked them for were worth showing up for.
