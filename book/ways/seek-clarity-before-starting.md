
# Seek clarity before starting

We once launched a series called Tech Talks. The execution was clean. Zoom links generated, calendars blocked, mentors chasing students to attend. Midway through, we noticed the students were bored and the content was generic. We had complete clarity on the logistics and none on the question that mattered: why does this matter for the student? The sessions all ran, and no student was better off for them.

The order that prevents this is why, then what, then how. First be able to say why this grows the student. Then describe what success will look like when it works. Only after both of those, the dates and the links. Start from the how and you can execute a pointless thing perfectly.

Our own placement training taught us the same lesson. In 2022 we planned the training and ran it. Only in 2023 did we start with the question "what is this student's baseline", and diagnose before training. The fundamental step was missing because we started with the plan, not the why.

Two things stop people from asking. One is speed, the worry that questions delay the start. But directional clarity is not a 50-page manual. You do not need the exact parking spot, you need to be certain you are driving north and not south, and you keep clarifying as you go. The other is conditioning. Many of us were schooled on "don't ask questions, do what the teacher says", and asking feels like admitting incompetence. Here it signals the opposite. Someone who asks until they could explain the task's purpose to another person can now make the hundred small calls the task contains. Someone who executes without that understanding guesses at each of those calls, and the guesses are what gets redone.

Also say your assumptions out loud, because what each side thinks is obviously understood is usually the thing they understand differently. Saying it costs a minute, and someone in the room may also tell you the problem is already solved on another team.

Our side: questions get answered without judgment, at any stage. The moment asking why is treated as slowness, people stop asking, and we go back to perfect execution of the wrong things.

What are you working on right now that you could not explain the why of?
