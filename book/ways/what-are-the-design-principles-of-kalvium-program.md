
# What are the design principles of Kalvium program?

Someone proposes a new module for next semester. It is interesting, the content is solid, a partner university likes it. Do you add it?

Our test is one question: how will this help a student become a 10x engineer, in life and not just in code? We mean 10x in skill, values, discipline, mindset and joy together, and we leave the full definition open on purpose. If a module cannot answer that question, it does not go in, however interesting it is.

The design principles follow from that test. The program is project-heavy and application-driven, because students learn in the building. It is work-integrated, so internships and real industry tasks sit inside the program rather than after it. Deadlines, teamwork, documentation and reviews are part of the curriculum, because those are the behaviours a company will judge them on. We measure mastery rather than rank, through progress checks and mentor feedback, instead of one score at the end. Self-learning is scaffolded, so that a student keeps learning after they graduate and nobody is teaching them. We nudge, we do not spoon-feed. And the design iterates on feedback from delivery, from student outcomes, and from the companies our students work with.

What we refuse to be matters as much. We are not a placement coaching center, not a way for students to earn during college, and not a course catalog that chases whatever the market asks for this year. When breadth and depth compete, we design for depth.

Our side: when a session is broken, the design is accountable, not just the mentor delivering it. Feedback from the ground must reach the designers and change the next iteration.

Next time you see any hour on the timetable, ask the question yourself. Which part of the 10x engineer is this hour building?
