
# Surface issues. Calling out misses and failures is a sign of growth, not incompetence

A faulty question turns up in an assessment. The person who spots it quietly fixes the marks for the one student who complained, closes the ticket, and moves on. Considerate, fast, and wrong. The bad question came from somewhere, and because nobody surfaced it, whatever produced it keeps producing. The next cohort gets the same faulty question, and a five-minute flag has become a recurring cost.

We hold one rule here: mistakes are okay, crime is not. A mistake is trying your best and getting the outcome wrong, which is normal in a program pushing this hard. The crime is hiding the mistake out of fear or ego. Mistakes are how we learn. Hiding them is how systems decay.

So the order of operations is fixed: first surface, then solve, then update. Raise the issue where the relevant teams can see it before you patch it locally, because the fix should hit the root cause, not the symptom in front of you. A pattern flagged loudly and early gets fixed once. A pattern patched quietly gets patched again by someone else next term.

Surfacing takes courage, and mostly the courage to look less competent for a moment. Read it the other way: the person who says "this is broken, and I may have broken it" has just protected the students who come after. We celebrate that person. If raising a flag on your campus earns anyone a reprimand instead of thanks, that is itself an issue, and it goes to your manager or beyond until someone acts.
