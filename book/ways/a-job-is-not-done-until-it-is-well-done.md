
# A job is not done until it is well done

You have thirty student records to update in HEROS, our student tracking system, and a meeting in ten minutes. For four of them you are not sure about the attendance number. Do you put in your best guess and move on, or do you go back and check?

Nobody will know. That is the actual condition of most of our work.

A young Steve Jobs was building a fence with his father and asked why they had to sand and paint the back of it, the side nobody would ever see. His father said: "But you will know."

Most of what we do has a back of the fence. The data quality in HEROS that no student ever sees, but every intervention we make on that student depends on. The session you prepared for properly versus the one you walked into. The email you read twice before sending. Nobody inspects any of these.

Our promise to a student is delivered through a few hundred small acts: sessions, records, reviews, plans, messages. A guess in a student record leads to a wrong conversation with that student in three weeks. A rushed session leads to a class that stops paying attention. The work that nobody checks is what decides whether we keep the promise.

This is not perfectionism, which is polishing past the point where anyone benefits. The test is simpler. Would you be comfortable if the person receiving this work watched you make it?

Students copy the standard they see us practice. Think about what yours has been teaching them this week.
