
# Kalvium system should have student 360

A mentor sits down to talk to a struggling student. What does the mentor know? What they personally observed in class, plus whatever they remember hearing. The student's attendance history sits in one tool, assessment results in another, internship progress with a different team, and the note from last semester's mentor left with that mentor.

Acting on one fragment produces the wrong conversation. The attendance record alone says push harder, and the mentor who saw the lab hours knows the effort is already high. Which conversation the student gets depends on which fragment their helper happened to hold.

So we hold this as a design requirement for the Kalvium system: a student 360. The learning journey is fully digital, which means what we learn about a student can be aggregated at the student level, across all facets, into intelligence a person can actually consume. And it must be visible to the mentors and Campus Managers who act on it, not buried in a database that requires a request to someone in another team.

The reason this is philosophy and not just product design: we promise to meet each student as one person. That takes the full picture in one place, and it should not depend on how long the helper has known the student. The mentor of two days should start where the mentor of two years would.

Our side of the deal: collecting this much about a student is justified only by using it for the student. The 360 exists to help them grow. That purpose is the test for what goes into it, and it bounds who sees it: the people helping that student, not everyone curious.

Before your next difficult student conversation, how much of their picture do you actually have?
