
# 7 growth pillars

Ask what a student is really made of by graduation day and a grade sheet answers a fraction of it. We name the whole of it in seven pillars, and since the names carry weight, here is what each one holds.

**Foundational Learning.** The base of a well-rounded person: awareness of self and the world, broadened perspective. Without it a student cannot keep up with a world that keeps changing.

**Computer Science Readiness.** Depth and fluency in CS through real practice: projects, coding challenges, public work like GitHub, going beyond what class demanded. The core craft of an engineer who can build and ship.

**Professional Readiness.** How a student shows up: communication, collaboration, punctuality, reliability, work ethic. Even strong coders struggle in internships and jobs without these.

**Productivity & Self-Management.** Running your own time, goals, energy and learning. Includes learning agility, picking up new tools and skills quickly. What long-term growth runs on once nobody is scheduling your day.

**Work Integration.** Learning proven outside the classroom: internships, open source, freelance work, research. The test of whether learning translates into real-world value.

**Community & Leadership.** Mentoring peers, leading clubs, organising, improving the place for others. We are cultivating people who shape culture, not just engineers.

**Values & Ethics.** Choices made with integrity and awareness of impact, visible in dilemmas, reflection and daily conduct.

Two habits for using the pillars well. Read them together: strength on two pillars does not answer for the other five, and the flat pillars tell you where to work with that student next. And name them to students, because a student who knows the seven dimensions can want to grow on them, while a student who only knows marks will optimise marks.
