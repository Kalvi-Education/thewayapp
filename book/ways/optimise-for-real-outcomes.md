
# Don't optimise for targets, optimise for real outcomes

Picture a campus manager whose dashboard is entirely green. Attendance above target. Sessions delivered on schedule. Support tickets closed on time. By every measure we gave him, he is doing well.

Then ask him which of his students are struggling, and with what. If he cannot answer, the dashboard has been lying to both of you. We have had versions of this happen.

He was managing the numbers, which is what we asked him to do. His mentors picked up the signal and started managing the numbers too. Attendance went up because attendance was being chased, not because more learning was happening in the room. The thing the number was supposed to represent quietly dropped out of the system, and nobody noticed, because the number still looked right.

This has a name, Goodhart's law, and it is worth knowing because it will happen to you too. A measure works while it is a side effect of the real work. The moment it becomes the goal, people optimise the measure directly, and it stops telling you anything. Attendance was meant to indicate engagement. Chase attendance on its own and you get a campus that reports well and teaches less.

The outcomes we care about are slow and hard to count. Whether a student can build something and then explain why they built it that way. Whether a mentor is better this semester than last. Whether a parent sends us their second child.

Dashboards are still useful. Use them to decide where to look, then go and look.

Run this test on yourself right now. Can you name your struggling students, without opening anything, and say what each of them needs next? If you can, the numbers will follow. If you cannot, your green dashboard is measuring something other than your students.
