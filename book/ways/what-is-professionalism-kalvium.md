
# What is professionalism at Kalvium?

Our mentors are young, often only a few years older than the students they guide, sometimes sharing a PG (paying guest accommodation) with colleagues who were their own college batchmates. The small age gap is why students relate to us. It is also a trap, because campus life can quietly turn into College 2.0, a workplace that still runs on college habits, where nothing feels like a big deal because it never did back then.

The big deal is this: you are not their senior. You are their educator. We grade students on professionalism, so our own bar is student professionalism plus. If we ask them to be on time, we are early. We cannot grade them on what we do not practice.

When a line gets crossed, the defence is usually "I didn't know." I didn't know I shouldn't take a lift from a student. I didn't know I couldn't call my colleague by his college nickname in the staff room. I didn't know I shouldn't comment on my students' party photos on Instagram.

We could answer with a rulebook. Rule 412, do not sit on a student's bike. But no rulebook covers next week's situation, and a campus where every interaction needs a rule has already lost the thing the rules were protecting. So instead of asking "is there a rule against this", ask "does this look like an educator". The distinction that helps most: be approachable, not friendly. Approachable means a student in trouble comes to you and is heard without judgment, and there is warmth in it, you can celebrate a student's win or ask how their week went. Friendly means becoming their peer, and a peer cannot correct behaviour or grade fairly without being accused of bias.

For everything else, three tests. Would you be comfortable if this conversation was pinned on the campus notice board? Would you say it if the person you are talking about was standing next to you? If a parent walked in right now, would they leave their child in your care?

Which of your current habits would fail one of those three?
