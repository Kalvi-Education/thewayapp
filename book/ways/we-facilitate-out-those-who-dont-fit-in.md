
# We facilitate out those who don't fit in

Someone on your team is capable and likeable, and is working against what we are building. Commitments slip, accountability is dodged, the work is treated as a transaction. Everyone around them sees it. What do we do?

First, separate two cases that look alike. A person who is struggling has a growth problem, and what they need is clearer feedback, better support, and real conditions to improve. A person who does not fit disagrees with how we work and acts accordingly, however skilled they are. We invest in the first and we part ways with the second. Getting this wrong is expensive in both directions. Exit someone who was only struggling and we lose a person who could have become a Kalvian. Keep someone who is not aligned and the people around them learn that the standards are optional.

Fit is judged in order: values, vision alignment, hunger, and only then capability. Skill sits last on purpose. It is the easiest of the four to build and the worst reason to keep someone who erodes the other three.

Before any exit, we hold ourselves to a clean conscience standard. Expectations were stated, not hinted. Feedback came early enough to act on. Real support was given. A genuine chance to change was given. The pattern continued anyway. If any of those is a no, the failure is ours first, and the exit is not ready.

Once the answer is clear, we move quickly. Dragging out a decision everyone can see coming spares us one uncomfortable conversation, and costs the people holding the line every day. The conversation is direct: the person hears the reason, knows what happens next, and is treated with respect on the way out.

If you are carrying a case like this right now, walk the standard honestly. Which check is still a no, and what are you doing about it?
