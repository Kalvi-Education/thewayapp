
# You are a Kalvian first

"Am I a student of this University? Or am I an intern at this Company? Or am I a Kalvian?"

Watch how students use these layers. When University exams approach: "But we are Kalvians, why do we have to follow University rules?" When Kalvium demands rigor and submissions: "But I am an intern now, you cannot tell me what to do." Whichever identity asks something hard right now is the one the student steps out of.

The world does not track these switches. A large multinational's India engineering centre decided to trust us and hired 36 students. That batch worked their hearts out and the company came back the next year wanting more. Some students in the next batch treated themselves as individual interns, dodged manager calls, came late, offered excuses. The company had no way to tell strong batch from weak batch in advance. What it could adjust was how much it trusted the name Kalvium, and it did.

The same misreading happens with complaints. A student once aired grievances about Kalvium's internal processes to the manager of the company he was interning at. Whatever sympathy that bought him, he had handed the manager a question: if he talks this way about the institution that built him, what will he say about us when he leaves?

For mentors, the identity question has a sharper edge. Deliver a hard decision with "the central team has instructed me to do this" and you have stepped out of the Kalvian identity in front of a student, exactly the move we ask students not to make. To the student, you are Kalvium. Own the decision: "We are doing this because it is necessary for your growth."

You cannot ask a student to be a Kalvian first if you are acting like a Kalvian second.
