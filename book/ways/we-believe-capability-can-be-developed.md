
# We believe capability can be developed

Is capability fixed at birth, or can it be developed? We believe it can be developed.

This idea is widely known as the growth mindset, and we deliberately do not lead with that phrase, because many people hear it as something about ambition, growing, earning more, thinking big. The belief is narrower and more useful than that. It is a claim about what a person who cannot do something today can become.

Say the sentence "this student is bad at math". Now try to plan what to do next. There is nothing to plan, because the sentence already delivered the verdict: nothing will help, that is how he is. The mentor stops looking for a way in. The student, who heard the verdict in some form, stops looking too. The judgment is a gate, and once it closes, all the useful questions (what specifically is he missing, what would move him this month, which explanation has not been tried) are on the other side of it.

Our duty is to keep those gates out of the system. Do not speak a verdict about a student in a review, and do not let a student speak one about themselves. The working tool is one word. "I can't do recursion" gets answered with "yet". Then the yet gets a next step attached, which concept is missing, what to try this week, because a yet with no plan behind it decays into a slogan.

The belief is not a promise. Capability develops through effort, good method and time, and a student may choose not to put those in. What the belief does is keep the gate open so the effort can count.

Listen for the verdicts in your own speech this week. Every "he is just not a coder" is a gate you closed on someone. What would you try if you had to reopen it?
