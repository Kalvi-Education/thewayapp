
# How to handle a university stakeholder (for campus teams)

The registrar wants your students at a university function during your assessment window. The exam cell wants your evaluation format changed to match theirs. The facilities office reassigned your lab, effective Monday. All three arrive friendly, and all three need different answers.

The principle that sorts them: clarity of roles, warmth of relationship. The university owns the ground, infrastructure, administration, compliance, and campus life our students fully belong to. Kalvium owns the program, curriculum, pedagogy and evaluation. Requests that live on their side of the line deserve flexibility and goodwill. Requests that cross into ours get a respectful, early, explained no.

So the function invitation is theirs to make and worth accommodating where you can, because our students are part of that campus, not guests on it. The evaluation format is ours, and the answer is no with the why attached. The lab reassignment is theirs to decide and yours to negotiate hard, because the program's delivery depends on it.

Two working habits keep this healthy. Invest in the relationship before you need it. A stakeholder who knows you and trusts you resolves quickly what a stranger turns into a formal escalation. And never let the university hear a no first from a student or a parent. Bad news travels through you.

When a request crosses the line repeatedly, do not keep absorbing the pressure locally. Raise it inside Kalvium, because a boundary defended only by one person's stamina is not a boundary, and a pattern of boundary-crossing is something the partnership itself needs to address.

Think of your university counterparts today. Which relationship is strong enough to survive your next no, and which one do you need to start building before it is tested?
