
# Students should respect each other

"It was just a joke, why are they taking it so seriously?"

You will hear this after a comment about someone's gender, someone's accent, someone's coding speed. The defence points at intent, and intent is not what we judge. We judge impact.

The reason is mechanical, not sentimental. The program is hard. Sprints are demanding, and the transformation we ask of students is uncomfortable on a normal day. A student who also feels judged or excluded because of who they are spends their attention managing that instead of learning. That student's learning suffers, and the system has failed them even though every session ran on time.

There is a useful idea from the discipline itself: the compiler does not care about your gender, your caste, your religion, or whether you grew up in a metro or a village. If the logic is sound, the code runs. Judgment by the quality of your work, and by nothing else about you, is the standard we hold the classroom to.

Two patterns to act on early. First, exclusion by background. Some students have coded since age 10 and some touched a laptop for the first time last year. Some speak fluent English, some are learning it. A professional helps the person catch up, not mocks their starting point. Second, exclusion of women. Tech has been unwelcoming to women for a long time and we are deliberately correcting that, so dismissive language and boys-club behaviour get named and stopped in the moment, not laughed off.

A test worth teaching students to run on themselves before they speak: does this make it easier for the other person to grow, or am I becoming a barrier to their learning?

Our side of the deal: when a student reports disrespect, we act on it. If reports go nowhere, students stop reporting, and the behaviour continues out of our sight.

The smartest coder in your classroom, do peers ask them questions freely, or are they afraid to?
