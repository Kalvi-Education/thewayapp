
# Involve students in Kalvium building?

Are we building Kalvium for students, or with them?

In a traditional college the student is a customer: pay, attend, consume, leave, and complain when the service disappoints. A builder responds differently to the same flaw, they fix it. Psychology has a name for why, the IKEA effect: people value what they helped make far beyond its parts. A student who mentors juniors, builds a campus tool, or reviews course content for clarity stops seeing Kalvium as a service and starts seeing something they are partly responsible for. That sense of being responsible for the place is what we are after.

But involving students is not handing over the keys. The framework is a 2x2, learning value against operational risk, and it sorts every opportunity into one of four responses.

High learning, low risk: say yes readily. Peer mentorship, internal tools, content review, community events. Seek these out. High learning, high risk, live production code, critical logistics: treat as an apprenticeship, offered to students who are thriving this semester, under close supervision, as an earned privilege. Low learning, low risk: refuse. Data entry and errand-running teach little, and saving staff time is not a reason to spend a student's. Low learning, high risk, grading peers, handling sensitive data: stays with staff.

Two boundaries keep the door honest. Feedback is a right, involvement is earned. Every student gets a voice, but a seat at the table where things get built comes from merit and demonstrated values, not from paying fees. And when a student does build for Kalvium, treat them as a professional. Same standards of delivery and accountability as an employee. Lowering the bar because they are learning insults the point of involving them.

We involve students to deepen their transformation, not to lighten our workload. Which of your current student volunteers would fail that test?
