
# Zeroeth value of students?

Eklavya wanted to learn archery. The teacher he chose turned him away. He built a clay likeness of that teacher, practised in front of it, and became an archer anyway.

We recommend this as the zeroeth value: the student who arrives already willing to learn, whether or not someone is teaching them.

Zeroeth, not first, because it sits before the list rather than on it. Integrity, discipline and professionalism can all be taught here. None of them can be taught to a student who is waiting to be taught. That student will attend, submit, and leave four years later carrying a degree and very little else, while a classmate with weaker marks builds themselves into someone companies want.

This is what selection is really looking for, underneath the scores. Not talent. The willingness to go at something without being handed it.

One caution about the story. Eklavya was turned away, and then asked to cut off his thumb. That part is not the lesson. A student should not have to succeed despite us. If a student here is learning like Eklavya, in spite of the mentor rather than because of one, that is a failure on our side, and it is worth finding out why before you admire their self-reliance.

Look at a student you rate highly. Are they learning from what we built, or around it?
