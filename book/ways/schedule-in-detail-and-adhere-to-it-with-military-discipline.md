
# Schedule in detail and adhere to it with military discipline

"When are the exams?" "When does the semester actually end?" "Do I have time to go home for Diwali?"

When students ask these in week six, something upstream failed. Ask yourself what it is like to work hard inside a semester whose shape you do not know. Every unanswered date is a question the student keeps carrying, and the worrying eats attention that should be going into learning.

So we schedule in detail, week by week, milestone by milestone, and publish it. Not because we love calendars. A student who knows exactly what is coming can stop worrying about logistics and put everything into the work. We absorb the ambiguity so they do not have to. That is our side of the deal, and it comes with a second half: the schedule we publish must be feasible. A calendar that overpromises and slips every fortnight teaches students to ignore calendars, which is worse than having none.

Detail is half of it. The other half is holding the schedule once it is out. Work expands to fill the time available, which means a small slip compounds: the test moved from Week 2 lands on the topic in Week 3, which pushes the project, and by Week 12 there are three weeks of syllabus left and one week of time. Then rigor gets cut in a panic, and the students pay for our slip.

Military discipline means the time is set, the expectation is clear, and the schedule binds everyone the same way: the central team that set it, the Campus Manager who runs it, the mentor who grades inside it, the student who submits inside it. When a mentor has to chase a student to submit, or a CM has to chase a mentor to grade, someone has learned that another person owns their time. Treat the published schedule as a commitment you made, because it is one.

And when life genuinely intervenes, a bandh, a cyclone, a campus shutdown, we recover inside the same week. Milestones do not move. Buffers exist so that a lost day is absorbed, not rippled forward. Pretending the disruption did not happen is not discipline, and neither is letting one lost Tuesday quietly push the whole semester.

Pull up your campus calendar. Could a student plan their Diwali travel from it today?
