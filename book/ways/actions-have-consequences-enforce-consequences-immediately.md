
# Actions have consequences - Enforce consequences immediately

A student walks in 15 minutes late and the mentor says nothing. The whole squad watched that, and what they learned is that punctuality is optional here. A copied assignment gets a grade, and the lesson lands: only output matters.

A violation seen and let pass becomes the new standard, because silence reads as permission.

Why immediately, and not after the semester? Because we are in the business of transformation, and transformation needs a tight feedback loop. A consequence tied to the action in time teaches. The same consequence three months later feels like bureaucracy, and the lesson is gone.

Delay also creates operational disasters. In one instance, students cheated in a company qualification exam. The team noticed but hesitated, wanting to verify everything first, and by the time the decision came, one student had cleared the interview and held the internship. Now every option was bad: ignore the cheating and kill our values, or pull a placed student and damage the student and the company relationship at once. A prompt response would have been a simple disciplinary matter.

Immediate does not mean harsh, and it does not mean skipping fairness. It means the behaviour is named now, the process starts now, and the student knows now that it is being taken seriously. What the consequence is can still be proportionate, considered, and where possible restorative. What cannot wait is the naming.

It also means no false hope. When the rule is clear and a student pushes back, "let me check with the central team" signals that the rule is negotiable, and when the no finally arrives they feel cheated. If the rule is settled, say no today. A maybe that turns into next week's no teaches the student that pushing has odds.

Our side of the deal: rules and their consequences should be written and known before anyone needs them, so enforcement is never invention. And a mentor who holds the line should be able to expect the campus and the central team behind them. Where either is missing on your campus, raise it, because enforcement without them is improvisation.
