
# Make every course grow the student in some way

Courses accumulate on a timetable over years. A university requirement here, a subject that has always been taught there, a topic someone believed in years ago. Ask why a course exists and the answer is sometimes only that it does.

Our test for a course is one question: how does it grow the student? Three kinds of answer count. It builds a fundamental skill, the kind that stays true for decades. It builds a humanities or life skill, the kind that makes a fuller person and a better colleague. Or it builds a specific tech industry skill that is relevant now. A course can serve more than one. A course that serves none has no claim on the timetable, whatever its history.

Notice what the test does not accept: it has always been there, we had faculty free. Those are reasons a course exists, not reasons a student should spend their hours in it. A course the university genuinely requires is a different case, we may not be able to remove it, but the test still applies to how we teach it. Find the growth in it and teach towards that, rather than delivering it as an obligation.

The same question works at every scale. A module inside a course, a session inside a module. If you design or deliver any of it, you should be able to finish this sentence without pausing: this grows the student in ___.

Pick the course you know best. Which of the three kinds of growth is it delivering, and would its students give the same answer?
