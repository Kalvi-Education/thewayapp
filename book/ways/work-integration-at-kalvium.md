
# Work Integration at Kalvium

A classroom, however well designed, differs from work in ways a student cannot fully see from inside it. Deadlines are negotiable in ways a client's are not, problems arrive pre-scoped, and the cost of a wrong answer is a grade. A student can do well in classrooms for four years and still meet real work for the first time at their first job, where discovering the difference costs them an offer or a probation period instead of a course correction.

So we put real work inside the degree. From year 2, students work with companies, 30 to 40 hours a week of committed work, on problems the company actually needs solved. Not at the end of the program as a send-off. In the middle, where what the work exposes can still be fixed.

That word exposes is the point. An internship is where learning gets tested against reality: the code review from an engineer who does not grade on effort, the requirement that changes mid-sprint, the standup where you say what you finished and everyone hears it. Employer feedback flows back to us, and it tells both the student and the program where the gaps are, in time to act.

Two boundaries keep this honest. The work must be real: a student shadowing a team and building nothing is not work integration, and we say so to companies. And the student is not cheap labour: a company that wants output without mentorship, feedback or context is misusing the program, and we say no to that misalignment even when saying no costs us the partnership.

The reason this sits inside the degree rather than after it: a student who has already shipped for a real company interviews differently, works differently, and knows things about themselves that a classroom alone would not have surfaced.

For the students you know in year 2 and beyond, is their work teaching them, or just using them?
