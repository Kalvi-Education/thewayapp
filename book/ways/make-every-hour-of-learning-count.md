
# Make every hour of learning count

There is a comfortable belief in education: make the class interesting and students will automatically come, phones will stay in pockets, attention will follow. It is not true, and the reason is worth understanding.

The student is not choosing between a good class and a bad class. They are choosing between a good class and a good movie, a game, a reel, a nap. Instant gratification on one side, and on the other something that hurts to do today and pays off in years. Education is struggle. We are asking a student to come and struggle for one hour, and then do it again the next hour, 8 hours a day. And it is human tendency to procrastinate even on things we know matter.

So we do two things deliberately. We build systems that mandate presence and keep distraction out of the room, because the choice is too hard to leave to willpower alone, hour after hour. And then, since we have taken the student's hour by mandate, we owe them an hour that counts. That is the design bar for every scheduled hour: the session planned, the mentor prepared, a learning goal it serves, and a formative check, some quick way of confirming the goal was actually met rather than assuming it was.

The arithmetic that makes this serious: an unprepared hour in front of 30 or 50 students wastes 30 or 50 hours of mandated student time, in one sitting.

Take the last hour you were responsible for. Could you name the learning goal, and would the students' work show it was met?
