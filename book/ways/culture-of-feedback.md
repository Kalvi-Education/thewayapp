
# Culture of feedback

A colleague runs a session you think could have gone better. Do you tell them?

At Kalvium the answer is yes, and the reason is compounding. A person who regularly hears the truth about their work keeps adjusting, year after year. Deny them that and the same mistakes quietly repeat, because nobody in the building was willing to name them. So before worrying about how feedback sounds, make sure it flows at all.

Flow does not mean spray. A student in Coimbatore once described their week to us as a rant: "I was giving feedback to this person, then I was giving feedback to this other person." Nobody had asked. Unsolicited feedback to random people is not a culture of feedback, it is a person with opinions. So we build forums, a retro, a review, a scheduled one-on-one, and we consolidate what we have observed and bring it there, rather than correcting people in the corridor in the moment.

Giving feedback is a skill you are expected to develop, and it has a shape. Say what you observed. Say how it read or what it affected. Then make a request. "The session ran twenty minutes over, the last exercise got cut, could you dry-run the timing next time?" Notice that every part of that points at the work. Feedback aimed at who someone is, rather than what they did, mostly triggers defence instead of change.

Receiving it is the other half. The person who points out your miss is doing the culture's work, and the grace with which you take it decides how much truth you hear next time. Thank them, especially when they are junior to you.

Who around you has probably been holding back feedback you made expensive to give?
