
# Problem solve with The Way, The Rule and The Thing

Let us say students are coming late to your class, multiple times, despite warnings of lost attendance. How should you respond?

Think about the options. Maybe you can setup a fingerprint scanner. Maybe you can announce a policy over an email. Maybe you counsel a student on why coming on time matters?

We describe solutions to problems at three levels: the thing, the rule and the way. Things are what you do for a specific problem at a specific instance. Rules are when you call out policies, protocols, for how something will be organized, so as to avoid the problem. And the way is the belief that guides you.

Under pressure, most often the things come to our mind. "No running in the hallway". "All attendance will be via fingerprint scanner". "You can't sit in placements if you fail this test". Sometimes you make rules out of them: "All of these markers have to be met for placements". "Attendance below 80% means failing that course". But rarely do you sit with a student and explore the way: "A company wants this skill in you, or you will not be able to get an internship. Now tell me why this class is not working for you? You have not showed up for the past 5 sessions."

The more senior you want to be in the organization, the more your instinct should be developed towards the way.

Rules and things are not wrong. A rule lets a hundred mentors hold the same line. A scanner saves everyone time on a rule people already accept. What matters is the order. Install the scanner after the conversation and it is a convenience. Install it instead of the conversation and students will find the workaround by the end of the month.

The next time a problem lands on your desk, notice which of the three your mind reached for first, and how long it took to get to the way.
