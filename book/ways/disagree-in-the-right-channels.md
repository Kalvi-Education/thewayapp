
# Disagree in the right channels

You disagree with a decision. What happens next depends less on how right you are, and more on who you say it to.

We want disagreement here. A person who questions assumptions and challenges a decision is worth more to us than one who nods along. But whether your disagreement changes anything depends on where you take it.

Think about where a disagreement can go. To your reporting manager, who owns the decision or knows who does. Up the escalation path if you are not heard. Into a town hall or a feedback forum, where the people who set direction are listening. Or: into a large WhatsApp group, into a corridor conversation over coffee, or in front of students. Nobody in those places owns the decision, so what you have shared is the frustration, not the problem.

Venting to students is the worst of these. The decision stays as it was, and the students now doubt the program they are inside of, which is a cost they pay, not you.

We have watched this from the student side too. We once changed a continuous assessment to cover the full syllabus, so that students would revisit older material and retain it longer. We announced it on day 1 of the semester. Some students, instead of asking their mentors for the reasoning, escalated to university personnel who have no role in our academic design. The decision did not change, because the people they went to could not change it. The concern went where it could not be answered, and so it was never answered.

One more thing about the resolution. In a working team, you will sometimes not get what you argued for. That is not the channel failing you. The channel owes you a hearing and a reason. It does not owe you the outcome you argued for.

Our side of the deal: the channels have to work. If you raise something with your manager and it disappears, escalate it, and we treat that escalation as alignment, not rebellion.
